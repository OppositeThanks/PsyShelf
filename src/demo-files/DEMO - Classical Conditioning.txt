DEMO - Classical Conditioning
Original educational sample prepared for a PsyShelf user walkthrough.
2026-10-02 | Language: English | Topic: Learning psychology

CONCEPT
Classical conditioning describes learning an association between stimuli. A previously neutral cue can come to elicit a response after being paired with a stimulus that already elicits that response.

EXAMPLE
A dog hears a short tone before food arrives. After repeated pairings, the tone alone may elicit salivation. The food is the unconditioned stimulus; the tone becomes the conditioned stimulus.

KEY DISTINCTION
Classical conditioning concerns associations between stimuli. Operant conditioning concerns associations between behavior and its consequences.

READING QUESTION
Which cue predicts the event, and which response is being measured?
